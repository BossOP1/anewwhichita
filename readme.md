ANew Mental Health Wichita — Website Build Master Brief for Chitransh
🎯 The North Star
This website should feel like ANew Mental Health first, TMS second.

ANew is already an established, full-service mental health practice. The new website needs to communicate:

Warm. Welcoming. Natural. Sophisticated. Modern. Calm. Human. Local.

The patient experience should essentially say:

“You may not know exactly what kind of help you need. That’s okay. We’ll help you find your next step.”

This is NOT supposed to feel like a hard-selling mental health/TMS website. The site itself should guide and educate patients toward the appropriate care.

The practice/team — not a treatment modality — is the hero.

1. DESIGN DIRECTION — THIS IS VERY IMPORTANT
Resiliency = strongest design/UX reference
Wesley specifically liked several aspects of the Resiliency website:

Overall flow as you scroll
Conditions displayed visually and individually
Ability to quickly hover/click and learn more
Individual condition pages
Services naturally incorporated throughout the journey
Providers connected with treatments/conditions
TMS being “sprinkled in” instead of dominating the homepage
Clean, sophisticated presentation
So: take inspiration from Resiliency's thinking and flow, but DO NOT clone it.

Rashi also specifically emphasized that every MyKohi website should be its own product, even when we're borrowing ideas from another build.

Color direction
Keep the existing ANew logo and its blue/green identity.

However, Cari specifically does NOT want the entire website saturated in bright blue and lime green.

Instead:

White/clean space
Neutral/natural backgrounds
Soft earthy tones
Subtle blue/green brand accents
Calm rather than bright
Modern but not sterile
Clinical enough to establish credibility without feeling like a hospital
Home-like and approachable
Sophisticated/aesthetic
Think natural + calming + premium, with the logo colors used intentionally as accents.

ANew already has Schedule an Appointment, which Wesley wants preserved.

Who ANew is → Who they help → What the patient may be experiencing → How ANew can help → What the patient's next step is.

Do NOT lead with:

Then we intelligently introduce TMS throughout the journey.

4. “FIND YOUR NEXT STEP” SHOULD DRIVE THE UX
This may be the single biggest functional concept from the meeting.

A patient may know:

“I'm struggling.”

But NOT know:

“I need psychotherapy versus medication management versus TMS.”

The website should help them figure that out.

Build pathways around what the patient is experiencing, rather than expecting patients to understand clinical service categories.

For example:

I'm struggling with anxiety → Learn about anxiety → See how ANew treats it → Therapy / Medication Management / applicable treatment options → Meet appropriate providers → Schedule Appointment

Similarly, symptom-oriented pathways can help someone who doesn't even know their diagnosis.

Examples discussed included:

Trouble sleeping
Loss of interest
Persistent low mood
Trouble concentrating
Appetite changes
This is where the Tranquil Minds example also resonated with Wesley. He liked how symptoms can become the beginning of a pathway rather than requiring someone to arrive already knowing their diagnosis.

Important
Build this like guided self-education, NOT a sales funnel.

5. CONDITIONS WE TREAT
Give this substantial visual presence.

Wesley REALLY liked Resiliency's condition presentation.

Conditions should be:

Easy to scan
Visually attractive
Clickable
Connected to dedicated content/pages
Connected to applicable treatments
Connected, where appropriate, to relevant providers
The concept is:

“This sounds like me.” → click → learn → understand options → meet the people who can help → take action.

That creates both a strong patient UX and the SEO architecture discussed on the call.

6. TMS — IMPORTANT, BUT HANDLE CAREFULLY
TMS will be a separate entity operationally, but it will live within the ANew Wichita website.

It is also staying in the same suite as ANew.

Navigation
Under:

Services → TMS Therapy

Build a substantial TMS section/page.

TMS should include
What TMS is
Who may be a candidate
Conditions/applications supported by their actual clinical offering
Consultation pathway
FAQ
TMS quiz/self-assessment
Future physician/provider videos
Future educational videos
Strong but appropriate CTAs
Homepage
Highlight TMS as:

NEW / New Service / Now Offering TMS

But don't let it overpower ANew's existing identity.

The Resiliency approach is the best mental model:

Introduce TMS naturally in appropriate places so a patient reading about depression/anxiety/etc. can discover it.

That creates the conversation:

“I was reading about anxiety/depression on your website and saw something about TMS. Can you tell me more?”

That's exactly the kind of organic discovery Wesley responded positively to.

7. QUIZZES / SELF-ASSESSMENTS
This ties directly into “Find Your Next Step.”

They liked the idea of:

Mental health quiz
TMS quiz
Guided questions helping determine whether therapy, medication management, etc. might be an appropriate next conversation
The TMS quiz should be embedded/accessible within the TMS experience.

Do not frame these as diagnosing someone. They're navigation/education tools leading to the appropriate clinical conversation.

8. APPOINTMENT WORKFLOW — DO NOT BREAK WHAT ALREADY WORKS
This was a very clear request from Wesley.

They have a small, busy administrative team and do not want us creating a bunch of new lead channels or workflows.

Their current:

Schedule an Appointment → SimplePractice
works.

The patient fills out the inquiry → it feeds directly into their SimplePractice EHR → creates the potential client → alerts staff → staff routes/reviews accordingly.

KEEP THIS WORKFLOW.

Wesley can customize the SimplePractice inquiry.

He specifically mentioned adding TMS as an option alongside things such as Therapy and Medication Management.

So Chitransh should integrate the existing SimplePractice inquiry rather than replacing it with another website form/CRM pathway.

Someone who's anxious, depressed, overwhelmed, or simply confused shouldn't have to hunt through menus.
